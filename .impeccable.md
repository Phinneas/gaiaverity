# GaiaVerity — Design Context

## Design Context

### Users
Home gardeners working with real outdoor spaces — backyards, patios, small plots, challenging soils. Not hobbyist enthusiasts with greenhouses — people who want practical, trustworthy guidance that works in their specific context (Pacific Northwest, drought-prone climates, shady lots, clay soil). They come with a specific problem: mushrooms in the lawn, a bare slope, a fence line to fill.

### Brand Personality
**Grounded, earthy, curious.**
No hype, no fluff — real advice delivered with warmth. Like a knowledgeable neighbor who loves showing you what's growing in their yard. The voice is confident but never preachy. The site builds trust before it sells anything.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Feel:** Organic garden journal. Unhurried, rooted, tactile.
- **NOT:** Tech blog greens, sterile white, startup energy, stock-photo generic.
- **Mode:** Light mode primary. Warm, cream-toned backgrounds — not cold white.

### Typography
- Font: Inter Variable (already installed)
- Headlines: heavy weight, generous size, tight tracking
- Body: relaxed line-height, warm gray — never pure black on white

### Design Principles
1. **Trust before content.** The hero establishes what GaiaVerity is and why it matters before showing a post grid.
2. **Warm, never clinical.** Cream and tan backgrounds over pure white. Terracotta over electric blue for CTAs.
3. **Content earns its place.** Every section has a job — no filler, no decorative sections that don't serve the reader.
4. **Organic rhythm.** Sections breathe. Generous padding. Not everything aligned to a rigid grid.
5. **Soil-to-screen.** Visuals and copy should feel like they came from someone who has actual dirt under their fingernails.
