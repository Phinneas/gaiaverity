If you have started seeds indoors or kept houseplants, you have probably seen small dark flies hovering just above the soil. Many people assume they are fruit flies and set out a vinegar trap, but that does not work. Fungus gnats breed in soil instead of ripening produce and require a different approach.

## Identifying Fungus Gnats

Fungus gnats are dark gray to black, with long legs and a mosquito-like shape. They are thinner and darker than fruit flies. You will usually see them hovering an inch or two above the soil surface or crawling across it. If you disturb the soil of a potted plant and a small cloud lifts off, you likely have fungus gnats.

They are sometimes confused with fruit flies and drain flies. Fruit flies are rounder, tan or amber colored, and drawn to ripening produce. Drain flies are fuzzy and moth-shaped, and they breed in the biofilm inside pipes.

## Why They Show Up

Fungus gnats thrive in consistently damp soil. Adult females lay eggs in moist organic material at the surface, and the larvae feed on fungi, decaying roots, and organic matter. Plants watered on a fixed schedule often stay damp enough to keep the breeding cycle going indefinitely.

Seed trays are especially prone to fungus gnats because starting mix is designed to hold moisture. Bagged potting soil that has been sitting open can also arrive with eggs or larvae already present.

## The Life Cycle

An adult fungus gnat lives only about a week, but she can lay up to 300 eggs in the top two inches of damp soil. Eggs hatch within four to six days, and larvae feed for another two weeks before pupating. A population you notice today likely started three to four weeks ago.

Because the soil contains overlapping generations at different stages of development, a single treatment rarely resolves the issue.

## Control Methods Compared

| Method | How it works | Speed | Cost | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Yellow sticky traps | Attracts and traps adult gnats before they lay more eggs | Fast for adults, doesn't touch larvae alone | Low | Any active infestation, as a first response |
| Let soil dry between waterings | Removes the damp surface layer larvae need to survive | Slow, days to weeks | Free | Long-term prevention on established plants |
| BTI soil drench (mosquito bits) | Bacterial larvicide that kills larvae in the soil | Moderate, a few applications | Low | Larvae already established in soil |
| Diatomaceous earth | Dries out and kills larvae and adults on contact | Slow | Low | Combined with drying, not alone |
| Sand or cinnamon topper | Physical/chemical barrier that stops egg-laying at the surface | Preventive, not curative | Low | Seed trays and new plantings |

These methods work best in combination. Sticky traps catch the active adults, while a soil drench or drying schedule stops the next generation from developing.

## Using Yellow Sticky Traps

Adult fungus gnats are strongly attracted to the color yellow, making sticky traps an effective way to catch them.

The traps are coated in a slow-drying adhesive. When adults land on the yellow surface, they get stuck before they can lay eggs in the soil. You can use store-bought cards or make your own by coating a piece of yellow cardstock with a thin layer of petroleum jelly.

Push the trap into the soil at a slight angle so it sits an inch or two above the surface. For a houseplant collection, one trap per two to three pots is usually enough. For seed trays, lay a card flat just above the tray surface.

Traps typically begin filling within the first two to three days. It generally takes two to three weeks of consistent trapping combined with a drying schedule or soil drench to clear a population.

## Preventing Gnats During Seed Starting

Seedlings need consistent moisture while they establish, which creates ideal conditions for fungus gnats. A few adjustments can reduce the risk.

Bottom watering helps keep the surface layer dry. You can set trays in a shallow reservoir and let the soil draw water up. Once the seedlings are established, letting the top quarter inch of soil dry out between waterings can disrupt egg-laying.

Starting with a fresh, sealed bag of sterile mix reduces the chances of introducing eggs to your trays. Adding a thin layer of sand or ground cinnamon on the surface also makes it harder for adults to reach the damp soil.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do fungus gnats damage plants?

Adults do not damage plants. Larvae can feed on fine root hairs, which is mostly an issue for seedlings and young plants. Established houseplants can usually tolerate a moderate population.

### How long does it take to get rid of fungus gnats?

It takes about two to three weeks with consistent yellow sticky trap use combined with a drying schedule or soil drench. Traps catch adults quickly but do not stop new ones from developing in the soil.

### Will fungus gnats go away on their own?

They rarely go away on their own as long as the soil stays damp. Changing the watering pattern is usually required to end an infestation.

### Are fungus gnats the same as fruit flies?

No. Fungus gnats breed in soil, while fruit flies breed in ripening or decaying produce. A vinegar trap will not meaningfully reduce a fungus gnat population.

### Can I use yellow sticky traps outdoors in garden beds?

They are most effective in contained settings like pots and trays. In open garden beds, they catch other flying insects, including beneficial ones, so they are better reserved for houseplants and seed-starting setups.
