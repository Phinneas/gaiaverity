## What actually matters: the 5 specs that decide everything

### Food-grade material is the only spec that can hurt you

![Diagram: What actually matters: the 5 specs that decide everything](/diagrams/napkin-f6efb48fac.png)

The CDC recommends FDA-approved, food-grade containers. It also says never to use a container that held toxic chemicals, including bleach and pesticides. Those residues can transfer into the water. A container with no published food-grade claim isn't a candidate, no matter how cheap it's. If you can't confirm food-grade, the CDC's fallback is a tightly closing lid, unbreakable material rather than glass, and a narrow opening.

### Do the capacity math before you shop

The CDC's minimum is 1 gallon per person per day. That's 12 gallons for a family of four for 3 days, or 56 gallons for 2 weeks. Pick the number first, then pick the containers that add up to it. Shopping by container first tends to leave you short.

### Stacking when full

This is what separates a closet system from a floor pile. WaterBrick cross-stacks up to 4 ft advised. Reliance's Aqua-Stack goes 3 high when full. The 7-gallon Aqua-Tainer stacks only when empty, so a full one needs its own floor space.

### Full weight decides where it can live

Fill a heavy container where it will stay.

### Dispensing and opening

The CDC prefers a single small (5 to 8 cm) opening with a tight cover, which keeps hands and objects out. A hideaway spigot means you never lift a full container to pour. That matters more than capacity once a container is too heavy to move.

## FAQ

### How to store water in containers?

Clean and sanitize the container first, then fill it with water you know is safe and label it with the date. The CDC steps, as the drinking water guides describe them, are to wash with soap, rinse, then sanitize with 1 teaspoon of unscented liquid household bleach at 5% to 9% sodium hypochlorite mixed into 1 quart of water, shake, wait 30 seconds, pour out and air dry. Keep containers at 50°F to 70°F, out of direct sunlight and away from gasoline and pesticides, per the CDC. Replace self-filled water every 6 months.

### How long will 500 gallons of water last?

At the CDC minimum of 1 gallon per person per day, 500 gallons covers one person for 500 days. A family of four gets 125 days. Plain Prepper frames the same math smaller: 12 gallons for a family of four for 3 days, and 56 gallons for 2 weeks. So 500 gallons is far past the two-week goal most guidance sets. Plan for it to stay put.

### How long will water last in a storage container?

Six months is the standard for water you fill yourself. The CDC and Ready.gov both say to replace water that wasn't commercially bottled every 6 months, and the CDC repeats that figure for self-filled water. Store-bought water is different: follow the expiration date on the bottle. Reliance sets a shorter limit for its own containers, recommending you rotate water every 90 days. If a container has held bleach, pesticides or petroleum products, don't use it at all.

### Which container is best for water storage?

For most homes, a food-grade 55-gallon drum such as the Augason Farms drum is the pick World Water Reserve lands on, since one drum covers up to three people for two weeks at the CDC's 1 gallon per person per day. Drinking Water Filter takes the other view: buy several food-grade containers of 3.5 to 7 gallons rather than one large one. I would start small and stackable. A full drum weighs about 480 pounds and stays where you fill it, while a 3.5-gallon WaterBrick is 27 lb full.

### What's your PERMANENT water storage solution?

There is no permanent one, and I would skip anyone selling you a fill-it-once answer. Every refillable container needs rotation: the CDC says replace self-filled water every 6 months, and Reliance recommends every 90 days for its containers. What lasts is the system, not the water. Keep food-grade containers, clean and sanitize them before each fill, store them cool and dark, and treat commercially bottled water as the only kind you can leave alone until its expiration date.
