Most US lawn mushrooms are non-toxic to dogs, but four genera (Amanita, Galerina, Inocybe, Clitocybe) cause serious poisoning. Because field identification is unreliable, treat any ingestion as an emergency.

That's the short answer. The rest of this guide covers the species that actually matter, how fast symptoms appear, and the exact steps to take if your dog eats one.

## The Lawn Mushrooms That Matter: A Toxicity Table

Eight groups account for nearly every lawn mushroom a dog will encounter. They're ranked here from deadly to harmless.

| Mushroom | Toxin | Danger to dogs | Typical onset |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Amanita (death cap, destroying angel) | Amatoxins | Severe — liver failure | 6–24 hours |
| Galerina (deadly galerina) | Amatoxins | Severe — liver failure | 6–24 hours |
| Inocybe | Muscarine | Serious — nervous system | Minutes to 2 hours |
| Clitocybe | Muscarine | Serious — nervous system | Minutes to 2 hours |
| Chlorophyllum molybdites (green-spored parasol) | GI irritant | Moderate — vomiting, diarrhea | 1–3 hours |
| Agaricus (field/meadow mushrooms) | Generally none | Low — occasional GI upset | Hours, if at all |
| Coprinus / ink caps | Coprine (with alcohol) | Low — GI upset | Hours |
| Marasmius oreades (fairy ring) | None | None | — |

The two that do the most damage are the amatoxin producers — **Amanita** and **Galerina** — because their worst effects are delayed and can look like recovery before the liver fails.

## How Fast Do Symptoms Appear?

Onset time is one of the best clues to which toxin is at work:

- **Minutes to 2 hours** — muscarinic mushrooms (*Inocybe*, *Clitocybe*): drooling, vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal pain, pinpoint pupils, slow heart rate.
- **1 to 3 hours** — *Chlorophyllum molybdites*: profuse vomiting and watery diarrhea, often mistaken for "garbage gut."
- **6 to 12 hours** — amatoxin mushrooms (*Amanita*, *Galerina*): initial GI upset that may seem to resolve, followed by liver damage.
- **Delayed (24+ hours)** — liver failure: jaundice (yellow gums), lethargy, collapse, abnormal bleeding.

The 6-to-12-hour window is the dangerous one. A dog can appear to improve after the initial upset, then deteriorate as the liver toxin takes hold — which is why you never wait for symptoms to get worse.

## What to Do If Your Dog Eats a Mushroom

Treat any ingestion as an emergency, even if your dog looks fine.

1. **Remove any mushroom pieces** from the mouth.
2. **Collect a sample** — photograph the mushroom from top, side, and underside, and keep the whole specimen in a sealed paper bag (not plastic).
3. **Note the time of ingestion** and any symptoms as they start.
4. **Call for help** — ASPCA Animal Poison Control at **(888) 426-4435**, or your veterinarian, immediately. Expect a consultation fee with poison control.
5. **Don't induce vomiting** unless a vet or poison control tells you to.
6. **Go to an emergency clinic now** for vomiting, tremors, seizures, collapse, or yellow gums.

Give the vet your dog's weight, age, and medical history, plus the time of ingestion and the mushroom sample or photos. Those details speed up diagnosis and treatment.

## Preventing Mushroom Poisoning in Dogs

The safest mushroom is the one your dog never reaches.

- **Remove mushrooms before dogs get to them** — gloves on, pull at the base, seal in a bag, and bin it.
- **Reduce the conditions fungi like** — improve drainage, thin thatch, and remove decaying wood and leaf litter.
- **Teach a reliable "leave it" cue** and practice it in the yard during spring and fall.
- **Supervise during peak seasons** — mushrooms flush hardest after rain in spring and fall, which is exactly when most ingestions happen.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I know if a lawn mushroom is toxic to my dog?

You usually can't tell by sight alone — several harmless species closely resemble toxic ones. Rather than gamble on identification, treat every unknown mushroom as potentially toxic and keep your dog away until a vet or mycologist identifies it.

### What are the first signs of mushroom poisoning in dogs?

Early signs include drooling, vomiting, diarrhea, lethargy, and abdominal pain. The timing matters as much as the symptom: signs in the first two hours suggest a muscarinic mushroom, while a 6-to-12-hour delay before worsening points to liver toxins.

### How long after eating a toxic mushroom do symptoms appear?

It depends on the toxin. Muscarinic mushrooms (Inocybe, Clitocybe) cause signs within minutes to two hours. Chlorophyllum molybdites causes vomiting within one to three hours. Amatoxin mushrooms (Amanita, Galerina) cause serious liver damage 6 to 24 hours or more after ingestion, often after an apparent recovery.

### Should I make my dog vomit if it ate a mushroom?

No — not unless a veterinarian or poison control explicitly instructs you to. Inducing vomiting the wrong way can cause more harm, especially if the dog is already showing neurological signs.

### Who do I call if my dog eats a mushroom?

Call ASPCA Animal Poison Control at (888) 426-4435 or your veterinarian immediately. Both will guide next steps using the time of ingestion, the dog's weight, and any symptoms. Go to an emergency clinic for severe signs like seizures, collapse, or yellow gums.
